Introduction

This model considers non-residential facility capital upgrade projects as analogous to chemical reactions by evaluating facility energy related costs over four distinct phases: Pre-Retrofit (Baseline), Implementation, Commissioning, and Operation. The following four scenarios present different cannonical examples of conditions that commonly lead ZE retrofit project success (A) or failure (B,C,D)

1. Self-Financed Pathway (Cash Reserves)

Active: 100% Cash Hurdle (Ea)

The asset owner deploys working capital or cash reserves to fund net capital expenditures across the implementation period:

Operative Dynamics
Activation Barrier: Produces a severe, concentrated cash drain during implementation with monthly expenditures peaking at +$49.5k/mo above baseline.
Adoption Bottleneck: Even when strongly NPV positive, projects stall if owner liquidity or implementation downtime concerns are insurmountable issues.
Benefit Profile: Implementation J-curve deficit followed by 100% unencumbered retention of utility savings (+$60k/yr) post-commissioning.

2. Debt-Financed Pathway (Amortized Facility)

Amortized (Ea ≈ 0)

Debt financing acts as a financial catalyst, amortizing upfront Cap-Ex into fixed monthly debt service commencing at the start of the implementation period:

Operative Dynamics
Activation Barrier: Upfront balance sheet cash outlay is eliminated or reduced, leveling the peak expenditure mountain into flat monthly debt service.
Adoption Bottleneck: Project viability is governed by the Debt Service Coverage Ratio ($\text{DSCR}$). When $\text{DSCR} \ge 1.0\text{x}$, energy savings exceed debt service.
Benefit Profile: Upon loan maturity (Year 10), debt service drops to $0, unlocking full operational utility savings (+$60k/yr) through Year 20.
Underwriting Standards & Term Scaling WAUL Principle

In energy project finance, debt amortization periods scale with borrowing volume according to the Weighted Average Useful Life (WAUL) of installed mechanical assets. Matching term lengths to equipment durability flattens monthly debt service ($D$) below utility savings ($\Delta\text{Op-Ex}$), preserving cash flow and securing $\text{DSCR} \ge 1.0\text{x}$:

< $150k 5-Yr Term Lighting & Controls
$150k–$300k 10-Yr Term Unitary HVAC
$300k–$400k 15-Yr Term Hydronics & Pumps
≥ $400k 20-Yr Term C-PACE / Central Plants

Note: Default loan periods automatically scale to these tiers when adjusting Cap-Ex, and can be manually fine-tuned via the sidebar slider.
